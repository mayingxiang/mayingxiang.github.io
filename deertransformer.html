DEERTransformer

Reading protein distance distributions out of DEER spectroscopy with a transformer

Double electron–electron resonance (DEER) measures the dipolar coupling between two spin labels attached to a protein. The instrument returns a decaying, oscillating time-domain trace; what a structural biologist actually wants is P(r), the distribution of distances between the two labels. Recovering P(r) from the trace is an ill-posed inverse problem. The usual answers are to fit a small sum of Gaussians, or to apply Tikhonov regularisation. Both require the user to decide up front how many populations there are, or how smooth the answer should be.

DEERTransformer is an encoder–decoder transformer that learns the inversion directly from 1.5 million simulated traces. It takes the raw complex trace and emits a 256-bin distribution over distance in one forward pass, with no regularisation parameter to tune.

Eight ground-truth distributions from the standard DEER benchmark, chosen to span the range of shapes, from sharp single populations to overlapping pairs and broad multi-modal cases, and to include samples that each method handles best. This is the whole difficulty of DEER in one picture. All three fits sit inside the noise of the measured trace, so the time domain cannot tell them apart, yet they disagree about where the distance populations are. Which answer you get depends on what each method assumes, not on the data alone.

Method EMD (Å) How it works

Earth mover’s distance measures how far probability mass has to travel to turn one distribution into the other, in Ångströms. Lower is better. It punishes mass placed in the wrong region much harder than a slightly mis-shaped peak, so a method can look almost perfect on this plot and still score badly. Step the distance plot’s y-axis up to ×200 to see the small spurious populations that drive most of the difference.

Predictions on this page are pre-computed. Each trace was run offline through the trained checkpoint, through DEERNet and through Gaussian fitting; the page loads those saved outputs rather than running a model in your browser. The EMD values are the real ones and match the manuscript. Across the full 1353-sample benchmark, mean EMD is 0.092 Å (low noise) and 0.267 Å (high noise) for Gaussian fitting, 0.197 and 0.337 for DEERTransformer, and 0.282 and 0.477 for DEERNet.
